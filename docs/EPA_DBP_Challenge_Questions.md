## Answer Key

1. To improve public health protection by reducing exposure to disinfection byproducts.

2. Cancer and reproductive effects in lab animals; suggested bladder cancer and reproductive effects in humans.

3. All sizes of community water systems (CWSs) and nontransient noncommunity water systems (NTNCWSs) that add a disinfectant other than UV light or deliver disinfected water, plus TNCWSs that add chlorine dioxide.

4. Ultraviolet (UV) light disinfection.

5. Consecutive systems that deliver water treated with a disinfectant other than UV light.

6. Stage 1: December 16, 1998 (63 FR 69390). Stage 2: January 4, 2006 (71 FR 388).

7. Only when they add chlorine dioxide.

8. 40 CFR 141 (plus any approved state requirements).

9. 0.080 mg/L.

10. 0.060 mg/L.

11. 0.010 mg/L; applies to plants that use ozone.

12. Zero.

13. MCL 1.0 mg/L, MCLG 0.8 mg/L; applies to plants that use chlorine dioxide.

14. Chlorine: 4.0 mg/L as Cl2 (MRDLG 4). Chloramines: 4.0 mg/L as Cl2 (MRDLG 4).

15. 0.8 mg/L as ClO2 (MRDLG: 0.8 mg/L as ClO2).

16. No. Stage 2 left all MCLs and MRDLs unchanged. The main compliance change was RAA to LRAA for TTHM/HAA5, plus a new analytical method for bromate.

17. Chloroform, bromodichloromethane, dibromochloromethane, bromoform.

18. Monochloroacetic acid, dichloroacetic acid, trichloroacetic acid, monobromoacetic acid, and dibromoacetic acid.

19. Running annual average (RAA) of all samples from all monitoring locations across the entire system, compared against the MCL.

20. Locational running annual average (LRAA): each individual monitoring location's running annual average must not exceed the MCL. Stage 1 averaged across the whole system; Stage 2 holds every location to the standard independently.

21. Under RAA, a high result at one location could be diluted by low results elsewhere in the system. Under LRAA, no single location can hide behind system-wide averaging; every monitoring point must independently stay at or below the MCL.

22. RAA at the entry point to the distribution system. Compliance determination unchanged under Stage 2 (though a new analytical method was approved).

23. Chlorite compliance is based on the arithmetic average of each three-sample distribution-system set, whether collected as routine monthly monitoring or as triggered next-day monitoring. If the average of any three-sample set exceeds the 1.0 mg/L MCL, the system is in violation. A daily entrance sample above the MCL triggers the additional three-sample distribution-system set on the following day.

24. Stage 1 described the interval as monthly, while Stage 2 specifies every 30 days for TOC and alkalinity. Read together with the guide's unchanged-frequency statement, this is an interval clarification rather than a substantive change to the monitoring frequency or location requirements.

25. At the entry point to the distribution system, monthly. Applies to systems using ozone.

26. Daily at the entrance to the distribution system, plus monthly at 3 locations in the distribution system. Applies to systems using chlorine dioxide.

27. Daily, at the entry point to the distribution system.

28. Same locations and frequency as Revised Total Coliform Rule (RTCR) sampling.

29. Stage 1: at the location of maximum residence time (Subpart H systems serving at least 10,000 needed at least 25% of samples at max residence time, remainder representative of average residence time). Stage 2: locations based on Initial Distribution System Evaluation (IDSE) results.

30. July 1, 2010.

31. Monitor during the month of highest DBP concentrations. Systems on quarterly monitoring (except Subpart H serving 500–3,300) take dual sample sets every 90 days at each location; annual-monitoring systems and Subpart H 500–3,300 systems take individual TTHM and HAA5 samples at the locations of highest respective concentrations.

32. No. Under 40 CFR § 141.623(a), Stage 2 qualification requires TTHM LRAA ≤ 0.040 mg/L and HAA5 LRAA ≤ 0.030 mg/L at every monitoring location. The system exceeds both thresholds at one location, so its favorable system-wide RAA and qualifying TOC result do not make it eligible. EPA’s statement that eligibility remained unchanged describes the continuing numerical thresholds and general eligibility concept; it does not make the Stage 1 system-wide RAA the controlling Stage 2 calculation. Where the summary guide is ambiguous, the explicit Stage 2 regulation controls.

33. Through March 31, 2009, the Stage 1 criterion was a source-water bromide RAA < 0.05 mg/L. Beginning April 1, 2009, that criterion was replaced by a bromate RAA ≤ 0.0025 mg/L at the entrance to the distribution system, based on the most recent four quarters of monthly bromate measurements analyzed using an approved method.

34. The source-water annual-average TOC level before treatment must be ≤ 4.0 mg/L at each treatment plant treating surface water or ground water under the direct influence of surface water. TOC samples must be taken every 30 days to qualify; once qualified for reduced TTHM/HAA5 monitoring, a system may reduce source-water TOC monitoring to every 90 days and must continue to meet the annual-average limit.

35. OEL = (Q1 + Q2 + 2Q3) / 4, where Q1 and Q2 are the two previous quarters' TTHM or HAA5 results and Q3 is the current quarter's result at that location. Calculated for both TTHM and HAA5 at each monitoring location.

36. Exceeded during any quarter in which the OEL is greater than the TTHM or HAA5 MCL. The system must: (1) conduct an operational evaluation examining treatment and distribution practices that may contribute to TTHM/HAA5 formation, plus steps to minimize future exceedances; (2) submit a written report to the state within 90 days of being notified of the results that caused the exceedance; (3) keep a copy of the report and make it publicly available on request.

37. To reduce peaks in DBP levels and exposure to high DBP levels. Applies to all systems subject to Stage 2 DBPR monitoring that conduct compliance monitoring and collect samples quarterly.

38. No. If the plant is not practicing softening, the required Step 1 removal is 35.0% (the >4.0 to 8.0 mg/L TOC row and >60 to 120 mg/L alkalinity column). If it is practicing softening, the required removal is 25.0%, because softening systems must use the far-right >120 mg/L alkalinity column for the applicable TOC range regardless of measured alkalinity. The treatment mode must therefore be established before selecting the percentage.

39. Enhanced coagulation or enhanced softening. Systems practicing softening must use the far-right >120 mg/L alkalinity column for the applicable source-water TOC range regardless of their measured source-water alkalinity, unless an applicable alternative compliance criterion governs.

40. April 1, 2012 (Schedule 1).
